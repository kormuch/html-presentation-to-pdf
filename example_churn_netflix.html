The Churn Problem
What Is Predictive Analytics?

Definition

Using historical data and statistical models to estimate the probability of a future outcome — here: which customers will churn.
The Pipeline
Define target → Engineer features → Train model → Validate → Act

Key Challenges in Churn Prediction

  • Target definition: Voluntary cancellation? Non-renewal? Downgrade? Each produces a different model.
  • Class imbalance: At 2% monthly churn, 98% of labels are "stays" — accuracy is misleading, precision matters.
  • Label window: Predict churn within 30, 60, or 90 days? Too short = too late to act. Too long = noise.
  • Temporal validation: Train on past, test on future — never random split on time-series data.
  • Feature drift: Content library, pricing, and competition change — models need retraining.
Imani et al. (2025) · Artun & Levin (2015)

The Netflix Campaign
How Much Should You Spend to Keep a Customer?
X = Y% × LTV

The Formula

X Max spend per customer on retention
Y% % of at-risk customers saved by the campaign (measure via holdout group)
LTV Lifetime value of saved customers

Netflix Example

Monthly churn~2%
Estimated LTV (marginal)~$245 (24 mo × $12 ARM × ~85%)
24 mo = estimated median tenure · ~85% = marginal contribution (content is fixed cost; incremental subscriber cost ≈ $0)
Sensitivity: $180–$310 depending on tenure (18–30 mo) and margin (80–90%)
Campaign saves15% (DTC benchmark, Artun & Levin p. 178 — confirm via holdout)
Max spend / subscriber0.15 × $245 ≈ $37 (range: $27–47)
⚠ At 30% save rate: ~$74/subscriber — treat as ceiling, not target. Y% is unknown until measured via holdout.
For reactivation (next slides): replace LTV with SLTV (Second Lifetime Value) — reactivated customers have shorter expected tenures.
Source: Artun & Levin (2015), p. 178 · LTV adjusted to contribution margin

The Netflix Campaign
Step 1: Select the Right Customers.

SELECT

Exclude
  • Shared-account users
  • High discount history
  • Tenure under 3 months
Target
  • Former Premium subscribers
  • 12+ months tenure
  • High prior engagement
Why: Higher ARM (Average Revenue per Membership) + proven engagement = highest SLTV. Budget: ≤$37 LTV ceiling; actual SLTV-based budget lower (shorter second tenure).

SLTV — Second Lifetime Value

The predicted revenue a reactivated subscriber will generate during their second tenure — not to be confused with first-tenure LTV.
Why is SLTV lower than LTV?
  • Reactivated customers have shorter second tenures
  • Higher re-churn risk in months 1–3
  • Often return on lower-tier plans
Implication for campaign budget
LTV ceiling from Slide 9: ≤$37/subscriber. Reactivation budget must be below this — based on SLTV, not full LTV.
Artun & Levin → Netflix: what the retail framework does not capture
Rec-engine effects (80% of views) · Near-zero marginal cost (no inventory risk) · Password-sharing residual · Ad-tier upsell dynamics
Framework: Artun & Levin (2015), Ch. 13, pp. 171–182 — adapted for streaming · SLTV concept: Stauss & Friege (1999), Thomas et al. (2004)

The Netflix Campaign
Step 4: Customize. Match the Offer to the Reason.
The lapse reason determines the offer. This is the step most campaigns get wrong.

Content Gap

Signal: Content drought in preferred genres

Message: "We added [titles] in the genres you watched most"

No discount needed

Price Sensitivity

Signal: Cancelled after price increase

Offer: Ad-tier or temporary discount

Not a premium offer

Support Issue

Signal: Multiple support tickets

Action: Acknowledge, confirm fix, direct line

Specificity converts

Principle:
Monetary offers only for price-sensitive segment. Never as default. Using discounts as a default trains customers to churn for a deal.

The Netflix Campaign
How Do You Know It Works?

Holdout Design

  • Split at-risk population: 80% treatment, 20% control (no intervention)
  • Measure lift: treatment reactivation rate vs. control (natural return rate)
  • Y% = incremental reactivation rate = treatment return rate − control return rate
  • Minimum duration: 90 days to capture full effect
Without a holdout group, you cannot separate campaign effect from natural return rate. Every campaign needs one.

Success Metrics

Primary
  • Revenue retained, not subscriber count
  • Incremental reactivation rate (treatment return rate − control return rate)
  • Campaign ROI, revenue retained vs. cost
  • Second-tenure retention, months 1–3
Red flag: If reactivated customers churn again within 60 days, the campaign generates negative ROI.
214% ROI
on reactivation vs. 23% on new acquisition — but only after holdout validation confirms Y%.
Stauss & Friege (1999); Thomas et al. (2004)
Limitations
Three Risks That Can Kill the Campaign:

The Black-Box Problem

  • XGBoost predicts who will churn — not why
  • Score without reason → CRM team won't act
Fix: SHAP values (feature contribution scores per prediction) → surface top 3 drivers per customer.
Imani et al. (2025)

The Deal-Seeking Trap

  • Proactive plan offers increased churn from 6% to 10% over three months
  • Contact lowered switching inertia and raised attention to usage gaps
Fix: Contact only with clear value proposition. Content relevance first, financial offers only for price-sensitive segment.
Ascarza et al. (2016) — telecom field experiment

The Privacy Constraint

  • GDPR Art. 21(2): right to object to profiling for direct marketing
  • Art. 5(1)(c) data minimization: limits the features available to churn models
Implication: Churn models in EU markets must use only necessary data — restricts feature scope, not model class.
Alom et al. (2024) · Bujor & Ene (2025)
Limitations
Two More Risks — Targeting and Cost:

From Risk Scoring to Uplift

  • Slides 11–12 rank customers by return probability × SLTV — a pragmatic starting point
  • Ascarza (2018) shows the better question is not who will churn but who is persuadable
  • Risk-based targeting can spend on customers who return anyway — or prompt those who would have stayed
Next step: Uplift modeling — target on incremental treatment effect. Our RFM framework is the baseline; uplift refines who to contact, not who to score.
Ascarza (2018), Retention Futility, JMR 55(1)

The Cost of False Positives

  • Discounting subscribers who would have stayed anyway is the dominant campaign cost
  • Ascarza et al. (2016): proactive contact can lower switching inertia and raise attention to usage gaps
  • The $37 ceiling assumes perfect targeting — real spend must account for wasted interventions
Fix: Price false positives into the retention budget. Measure incremental reactivation rate via holdout, not gross return rate.
Ascarza et al. (2016) · Artun & Levin (2015)
Conclusion
What Would I Recommend to Netflix?
1

Measure dollar retention, not headcount.

Segment subscribers into Premium / Standard / Ad tiers. Track ARM per cohort quarterly. Reducing defections by 5% can increase profits by 25–85% — but only if you measure the right thing.

2

Deploy XGBoost with interpretable outputs.

Surface top 3 churn drivers per customer via SHAP values. Give CRM agents a reason, not just a score. Without that, the model sits unused.

3

Run a holdout-controlled reactivation pilot before scaling.

80/20 split, 90-day measurement window, validate Y% on your own population. One case study found 214% ROI on reactivation vs. 23% on acquisition — but only after you confirm your own save rate. Lead with content, not discounts.

The cost of inaction is measurable. The tools exist. The only variable left is whether you act on the data.
